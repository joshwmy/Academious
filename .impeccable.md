# Academious Design Direction

## Design Context

### Users

Academious is for lecturers, researchers and students who need to keep up with
a large, fast-moving body of academic work without repeatedly constructing
searches or navigating a traditional research database.

The product should support a quiet, repeatable reading ritual. Opening it in the
morning should feel as approachable as reading the news or doing a crossword:
the system has already assembled a useful, personalised selection, and the
reader can quickly decide what deserves attention.

The core job is passive research discovery: recommend relevant papers, make
their academic context immediately legible, let readers save useful work, and
help them refine their interests without adding noise.

### Brand Personality

**Focused, approachable, contemporary.**

Academious should feel academically credible without looking institutional,
and intelligent without feeling technical or intimidating. Its confidence
comes from clarity, restraint, useful personalisation and careful handling of
research metadata.

The emotional goal is calm momentum. Readers should feel that keeping current
with their field is manageable, rewarding and naturally part of their day.

### Aesthetic Direction

The visual direction is a **clean, minimalist academic SaaS workspace**. It
combines the calm workspace clarity of Notion, the restraint and precision of
Linear, and the passive personalisation of a Spotify-style discovery feed. The
result should be recognisably Academious rather than a direct imitation of any
reference product.

Use a light, desktop-first dashboard with generous whitespace, understated
typography and a persistent left navigation rail. The interface should be
primarily white and very light grey, with soft lavender, muted violet and
periwinkle blue used selectively for active states, tags and brand moments.
Use a clean sans-serif typeface similar in character to Inter, with a clear,
compact hierarchy optimised for scanning research titles and metadata.

Components should use modest corner radii, thin neutral borders, minimal
shadows and small outline icons. Research cards are the primary content unit:
white surfaces with comfortable padding, a strong paper title, authors,
publication source, relative publication date, topic tags and an unobtrusive
bookmark action. Topic tags use small rounded lavender pills.

The persistent sidebar contains, in order:

- Home
- Papers
- Collections
- Interests
- Profile
- Settings, anchored at the bottom

The active navigation item uses a pale lavender background. The default main
view begins with **Recommended for you** and the supporting line **Research
tailored to your interests.** Recommended papers appear as a single vertically
stacked feed rather than a dense grid or analytics dashboard.

The brand mark is a lowercase “a” inside a rounded square with a restrained
blue-to-purple gradient. This logo is the deliberate gradient exception; avoid
decorative gradients elsewhere. Pair it with a lowercase, dark-navy wordmark in
a modern sans-serif face.

#### Core Palette

| Role | Token | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary surface | White | `#FFFFFF` |
| App background | Off-white | `#F7F7FA` |
| Active and tag surface | Lavender | `#EEEEFF` |
| Primary accent | Violet | `#7770EC` |
| Primary text and wordmark | Navy | `#171B2E` |
| Secondary text | Grey | `#858895` |

These colours are inferred from a promotional mockup rather than sampled design
tokens. Calibrate them during implementation to preserve visual intent and meet
WCAG 2.2 AA contrast requirements.

Avoid:

- dense analytics, database tables or complex controls on the discovery view;
- oversized cards, heavy shadows, glassmorphism or decorative effects;
- loud social-feed engagement patterns, gamification and attention traps;
- excessive badges, icons or controls competing with paper titles;
- using lavender so broadly that active states and topic tags lose meaning;
- introducing unverified interactions or responsive behaviour as though they
  were present in the reference mockup.

### Design Principles

1. **Passive discovery comes first.** The default view should present useful,
   personalised research immediately, without requiring a search or setup task.
2. **Research cards carry the experience.** Titles lead; authors, source, date
   and topics provide fast context; bookmarking remains visible but quiet.
3. **Keep the feed calm.** Prefer one clear vertical reading flow, generous
   whitespace and progressive disclosure over competing modules and controls.
4. **Make personalisation understandable.** Interests and recommendation cues
   should feel useful and editable without exposing scores or model internals.
5. **Use colour with purpose.** Lavender and violet identify selection,
   categorisation and brand—not decoration.
6. **Preserve workspace clarity.** The sidebar gives stable orientation while
   the main area stays dedicated to discovery and reading.
7. **Accessibility is foundational.** Target WCAG 2.2 AA, preserve semantic
   structure and keyboard access, never rely on colour alone, and maintain
   visible focus and comfortable reading contrast.
8. **Extend the mockup thoughtfully.** Treat the supplied image as visual and
   structural direction, not proof of interactions or responsive behaviour.
   Validate those behaviours as the product is implemented.

### Reference Status

The source image is a promotional mockup rather than a verified capture of a
complete running frontend. Its layout, hierarchy and visual language are the
design reference. Interaction details, edge cases and responsive behaviour must
be designed and tested rather than inferred as existing functionality.
